Overview

A sentence is a group of words making complete sense, containing a Subject (doer) and a Predicate (action/state). The building blocks are the 8 Parts of Speech.

Nouns

Words used to name a person, place, thing, or idea. Five categories: Common, Proper, Collective, Abstract, Material.

Core Rules of Nouns

Rule 1: Proper as Common

A proper noun becomes a common noun when pluralized or preceded by an article.

There are two Hitlers in this house.
Kalidas is the Shakespeare of India.

Rule 2: Collective Nouns

Generally take a singular verb. Exception: If there is division/disagreement, use plural.

The group consists of five members.
The committee have taken their seats. (Individual action)

Singular vs. Plural Logic

Nouns behave differently regarding countability and verb agreement.

Category Examples Verb Agreement Note
Abstract as Collective Youth Plural "Youth are the pillars of the nation."
Material Nouns Brick, Stone, Wood Singular No plural form used for mass matter.
Correct: "Made of brick and stone."
Singular Only Advice, Grain, Hair, Information, Scenery Singular Never say "informations".
Plural Only Scissors, Biceps, Trousers, Earnings Plural "My trousers are new."
Deceptive Plural News, Physics, Economics, Summons Singular Looks plural (ends in s) but is singular.
"Physics is difficult."
Deceptive Singular Cattle, Police, People Plural Looks singular but is plural.
"Cattle are grazing."

Rule 9: Plural Amount = Singular Unit

When a plural noun represents a specific amount, distance, or weight as a whole, use a singular verb.

Ten thousand rupees are a large amount.
Ten thousand rupees is a large amount.

Possessive Case (Apostrophe)

flowchart LR A{Object Type} A -->|Living| B[Use 's] A -->|Non-Living| C[Use 'of'] A -->|Personified| B A -->|Plural ending in s| D[Use only '] B --> E[Men's Club] C --> F[Leg of the table] D --> G[Girls' hostel]
  • Compound Nouns: Add apostrophe to the last word. (e.g., Commander in chief's office).
  • Joint Possession: Add to the last noun only. (e.g., Amar and Anil's visit).
  • Personified Objects: Treated as living. (e.g., Death's icy hands, Moon's beauty).

Pronouns

Sequence of Pronouns

When multiple pronouns are used in a single sentence, the order matters based on the context.

2-3-1 Positive Idea

For general statements, praise, or awards.

  1. Second Person (You)
  2. Third Person (He/She/They)
  3. First Person (I/We)
You, he and I will be awarded.

1-2-3 Negative Idea

For confessing faults, punishment, or negative acts.

  1. First Person (I/We)
  2. Second Person (You)
  3. Third Person (He/She/They)
I, you and he will be punished.

Key Rules

Subject-Verb Agreement Logic

1. "And" Rule: Two nouns joined by "and" = Plural.
Exception: If they refer to the same person/thing = Singular.

The account officer and treasurer (one person) should be careful in his work.

2. "Each/Every" Rule: Preceded by Each/Every = Singular.

Every student and every teacher took his or her seat.

3. "Either...or / Neither...nor" Rule: Follow the noun nearest to the verb.

Either the manager or his subordinates failed in their duties.
Specific Pronoun Usage
  • It: Used for time, distance, weather, or emphasis. (e.g., "It is raining", "It is 10 o'clock").
  • One: Possessive is "one's". (e.g., "One should do one's duties").
  • Relative Pronouns:
    • Who: Subject (The manager who abused...)
    • Whom: Object (The student whom you wanted...) - often omitted.
    • That: For persons/things, essential clauses.
    • Which: Infants, objects, selection (Which of these...).
  • Reciprocal:
    • Each other: Two people.
    • One another: More than two.

Adjectives

Modifiers that describe nouns. They have three degrees of comparison.

Critical Adjective Rules

Double Comparatives

Comparing two qualities in the same person.

Use more instead of -er.

Rekha is wiser than intelligent.
Rekha is more wise than intelligent.

The "Any Other" Rule

When comparing one item to a group it belongs to, use "any other".

Gold is more precious than any metal.
Gold is more precious than any other metal.

Latin Comparatives (The "OR" ending)

Words ending in -or (Superior, Junior, Senior, Inferior, Prior) take to, not than.

He is senior than me.
He is senior to me.
This is preferable to that.

Absolute Adjectives

Qualities that cannot be graded. Do not use "more" or "most".

Unique, Perfect, Round, Chief, Universal, Dead, Empty

This is the most unique idea.
This is a unique idea.

Confusing Quantifiers

Word Usage Meaning
Few Countable (Plural) Hardly any (Negative)
A Few Countable (Plural) Some (Positive)
Little Uncountable (Singular) Hardly any (Negative)
A Little Uncountable (Singular) Some (Positive)
Farther Distance Physical distance
Further Addition "In addition to"

Sensation Verbs

Verbs related to senses (look, seem, taste, smell, sound) are followed by Adjectives, not Adverbs.

The soup smells deliciously.
The soup smells delicious.
He feels bad.

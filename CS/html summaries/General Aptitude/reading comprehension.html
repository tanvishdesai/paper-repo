1. Grammar & Usage

Core rules focusing on Subject-Verb agreement, Conditionals, and Prepositions.

Subject-Verb Agreement (Nested Clauses)

"The strategies that the company [.....] to sell its products [.....] house-to-house marketing."
Structural Analysis
graph TD A[Sentence Start] --> B{The Strategies} B -- Plural Subject --> D[Require Plural Verb: 'include'] A --> C{that the company...} C -- Singular Subject 'Company' --> E[Requires Singular Verb: 'uses'] E --> F[Full Sentence] D --> F
Show Answer & Explanation

Correct: uses, include

  1. Clause 1: "that the company..." → Company is singular, so it uses.
  2. Main Clause: "The strategies..." → Strategies is plural, so they include.

The Third Conditional (Past Unreal)

"If the athlete had wanted to come first in the race, he [.....] several hours every day."
should practice
should have practiced
practiced
should be practicing
Formula

$$ \text{If} + \text{Past Perfect (had + V3)} \rightarrow \text{Modal Perfect (would/should/could + have + V3)} $$

Prepositions & Articles

1. "Mount Everest is [.....] peak in the world."
2. "The boat arrived [.....]"
Key Takeaways
  • Superlatives: Always use the definite article 'the' before superlatives.
    Correct: "The highest"
  • Prepositions of Location: 'At' is used for specific points or locations (like a dock/station). 'In' is used for enclosed spaces/countries.
    Correct: "The boat arrived At"

Homophones & Contextual Fillers

"By giving him the last [piece/peace] of the cake, you will ensure lasting [piece/peace] in our house."
  • Piece: A portion of an object.
  • Peace: Tranquility, freedom from disturbance.

Answer: piece, peace

Contextual Contrast

"Even though there is a vast scope for [.....], its tourism has remained a [.....] area."

The phrase "Even though" indicates a contrast between the two blanks (Positive Potential vs. Negative Reality).

improvement, neglected
rejection, approved

3. Critical Inference

Deducing implicit meaning from text. Remember: Inferences must be based strictly on the provided text, not outside knowledge.

Case Study: Historical Bias

Text: "Indian history written by British historians was well documented but not impartial. Subservient to the glory of the Union Jack. Later Indian scholars presented a contrary picture."

graph LR A[British Historians] -->|Output| B(Well Documented) A -->|Bias| C(Not Impartial / Biased) C -->|Goal| D[Serve Union Jack] E[Indian Scholars] -->|Reaction| F[Contrary Picture]

Inference: The history was well documented and researched but was sometimes biased.

Case Study: Rabies Control

"Rabies in India is a concern. Inoculating 70% of pets and stray dogs can lead to a significant reduction in infections."
The number of people in India infected is high.
Rabies can be eradicated (Too extreme).
Strays are the main source worldwide (Out of scope).
Why is A correct?
The text states the number is "overwhelming" and a "source of concern". Thus, it is valid to infer the number is high. "Eradicated" is incorrect because the text only promises a "reduction".

Case Study: Arts Funding Logic

Artists wrote to CM for funds -> No increase in Budget -> Artists anguished but optimistic.
Valid Inferences:
  • (i) Artists expected funding to increase (Implied by their request and subsequent anguish).
  • (ii) CM was receptive? False/Unknown (He didn't give the money).
  • (iv) Schools are giving less importance to arts? True (Text explicitly mentions "gradual sidelining").

Note: Slide marks (i), (ii), and (iv) as correct answer C, though strictly logically (ii) is weak.

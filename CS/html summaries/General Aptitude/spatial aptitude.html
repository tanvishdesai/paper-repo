1. Logical Venn Diagrams

Formula Reference

While often categorized under logical reasoning, set theory forms the basis for grouping questions. For 3 overlapping sets ($A, B, C$):

$$ n(A \cup B \cup C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) - n(A \cap B) - n(B \cap C) - n(C \cap A) + n(A \cap B \cap C) $$

Visual Representation

venn A((Art)) B((Music)) C((Dance))

Key Terms

  • Exactly One: $n(A) - n(A \cap B) - n(A \cap C) + 2n(A \cap B \cap C)$ (conceptually removing overlaps).
  • Exactly Two: Sum of regions where only 2 circles overlap.
  • At least One: Union of all sets ($A \cup B \cup C$).
  • None: Total Population - Union.

2. Classification & Grouping

The goal is to analyze a set of geometric figures and identify patterns or "The Odd One Out".

Grouping Logic

Group figures into classes (usually 3 groups of 3 figures). Look for:

Criterion Example
Components Triangle within a Circle vs. Square within Square.
Number of Lines 3 lines (Triangle, Arrow) vs. 4 lines (Square, Rectangle).
Shading Fully shaded, half shaded, unshaded.
Orientation Rotated 45°, 90°, or 180°.

Odd One Out

Identify the figure that breaks the established pattern.

Example Logic: A, B, and D rotate Clockwise. C rotates Anti-Clockwise.

Pro Tip: Always check rotation direction (CW vs ACW) and the number of internal elements first.

Mirror Image Rules

  • Axis: Vertical ($Y$-axis).
  • Concept: Left becomes Right, Right becomes Left. Top and Bottom remain unchanged.
  • Symmetrical Letters (Unchanged): A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y.
  • Tricky Numbers: Note how '2', '5', and '7' flip.

Water Image Rules

  • Axis: Horizontal ($X$-axis).
  • Concept: Top becomes Bottom, Bottom becomes Top. Left and Right remain unchanged.
  • Symmetrical Letters (Unchanged): C, D, E, H, I, K, O, X.
  • Tip: Visualize the object flipping downwards over an edge.

4. Paper Folding (Transparent Sheet)

The Concept: A square transparent sheet with a pattern is folded along a dotted line. The pattern on the folded half is "mirrored" onto the stationary half.

Strategy:

  1. Identify the Fold Line (Dotted line).
  2. Identify the Stationary Side (The side that remains in the final options).
  3. Treat the Fold Line as a Mirror.
  4. Reflect the pattern from the moving side onto the stationary side.
  5. Superimpose the reflected pattern on the existing stationary pattern.

5. Paper Cutting & Unfolding

This is the reverse of folding. A paper is folded multiple times, cut, and then unfolded.

graph LR A[Start: Cut Folded Paper] --> B[Identify Last Fold] B --> C[Unfold = Reflect Pattern] C --> D[Mirror Axis = Fold Line] D --> E{More Folds?} E -- Yes --> B E -- No --> F[Final Pattern]

The "Reverse" Method

Work backwards from the final figure ($Z$) to the first ($X$).

  • Step 1: Look at the final cut ($Z$).
  • Step 2: Unfold ONCE. Imagine a mirror at the fold line. The cut is duplicated symmetrically across this line.
  • Step 3: Repeat for previous folds until the paper is fully open.
Shortcut: If a paper is folded into 4 quadrants ($2$ folds), a single cut usually results in 4 symmetrical shapes in the final answer (unless the cut is on the fold line itself).

6. General Solving Strategies

1. Elimination

Don't look for the right answer immediately. Look for the wrong ones. If a rotation is wrong in Option A, cross it out immediately.

2. Reference Point

Pick one specific element (e.g., a dot or an arrow) within a complex figure and track ONLY its movement/rotation across the sequence.

3. Axis Visualization

For mirror images, physically place your pen vertically on the screen. For water images, place it horizontally. Use this as a mental anchor.

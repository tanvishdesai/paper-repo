1. The "Del" Operator & Basic Operations

SCALAR to VECTORGradient ($\nabla \phi$)

Represents the rate of change. Points in the direction of steepest ascent.

$$ \nabla \phi = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}\hat{i} + \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y}\hat{j} + \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z}\hat{k} $$
  • $\phi(x,y,z) = c$ represents a Level Surface.
  • $\nabla \phi$ is the Normal Vector to that surface.

VECTOR to SCALARDivergence ($\nabla \cdot \vec{F}$)

Measures "outward flux" per unit volume. How much a field spreads out.

$$ \text{div } \vec{F} = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z} $$
Physical Meaning
  • Positive: Source (Fluid expanding)
  • Negative: Sink (Fluid compressing)
  • Zero: Solenoidal (Incompressible)

VECTOR to VECTORCurl ($\nabla \times \vec{F}$)

Measures rotation or circulation of the field.

$$ \nabla \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ F_1 & F_2 & F_3 \end{vmatrix} $$

If $\nabla \times \vec{F} = 0$, field is IRROTATIONAL (Conservative).

2. Derivatives & Geometry

Directional Derivative (D.D.)

Rate of change of $\phi$ at point $P$ in direction of vector $\vec{a}$.

$$ D.D. = \nabla \phi \cdot \hat{a} $$ $$ \text{where } \hat{a} = \frac{\vec{a}}{|\vec{a}|} $$

Max Value: $|\nabla \phi|$ (in direction of normal).

Geometric Vectors

Normal to Surface ($\vec{N}$) $$ \nabla \phi $$
Unit Normal ($\hat{n}$) $$ \frac{\nabla \phi}{|\nabla \phi|} $$
Tangent to Curve ($\vec{T}$) $$ \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt} \text{ where } \vec{r}(t) \text{ is param.} $$
Angle betw. Surfaces $$ \cos \theta = \frac{\nabla \phi_1 \cdot \nabla \phi_2}{|\nabla \phi_1| |\nabla \phi_2|} $$

3. Integral Calculus Types

Line Integral (1D)

Work done by force $\vec{F}$ moving along curve $C$.

$$ W = \int_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} $$

If $\vec{F} = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$:

$$ \int (F_1 dx + F_2 dy + F_3 dz) $$

If Conservative, depends only on endpoints.

Surface Integral (2D)

Flux of $\vec{F}$ passing through surface $S$.

$$ \Phi = \iint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n} ds $$

Projection Method:

$$ ds = \frac{dx dy}{|\hat{n} \cdot \hat{k}|} \text{ (on xy-plane)} $$

Volume Integral (3D)

Integration over a 3D region $V$.

$$ \iiint_V f(x,y,z) dx dy dz $$

Used often with Divergence Theorem.

4. The Big Three Theorems

These theorems convert integrals to easier forms (usually increasing dimension by 1 but simplifying the boundary).

Green's Theorem

2D Plane Closed Curve

Relates line integral to double integral over region $R$.

$$ \oint_C (M dx + N dy) = \iint_R \left(\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\right) dx dy $$

Condition: $C$ is closed, planar, counter-clockwise.

Area Application: Area $= \frac{1}{2}\oint (x dy - y dx)$.

Stokes' Theorem

3D Space Open Surface

Relates line integral to surface integral of Curl.

$$ \oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \vec{F}) \cdot \hat{n} ds $$

Interpretation: Sum of "microscopic" rotations = macroscopic circulation on boundary.

Gauss Divergence Theorem

3D Space Closed Surface

Relates surface integral (flux) to volume integral of Divergence.

$$ \oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n} ds = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F}) dv $$

Use when: Calculating flux over a closed object (Sphere, Cube).

5. Standard Problem Archetypes

Case 1: Flux through a Sphere

Given $\vec{F} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ over sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2=a^2$.

  1. Identify Closed Surface $\to$ Use Gauss Divergence.
  2. Calculate $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 1+1+1 = 3$.
  3. Integral becomes $\iiint 3 dv = 3 \times \text{Volume}$.
  4. Result: $3 \times \frac{4}{3}\pi a^3 = 4\pi a^3$.

Case 2: Work Done (Conservative)

Find work done moving particle from $(0,0,0)$ to $(1,1,1)$ in field $\vec{F}$.

  1. Check Curl: Is $\nabla \times \vec{F} = 0$?
  2. If Yes: Path doesn't matter.
    • Find Potential $\phi$ such that $\vec{F} = \nabla \phi$.
    • Work $= \phi(B) - \phi(A)$.
  3. If No: Parameterize the path (e.g., let $x=t, y=t^2, z=t^3$) and integrate directly.

Case 3: Surface Integral via Projection

Evaluate $\iint \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n} ds$ on plane $x+y+z=1$ in 1st octant.

  1. Find $\hat{n} = \frac{\nabla \phi}{|\nabla \phi|} = \frac{\hat{i}+\hat{j}+\hat{k}}{\sqrt{3}}$.
  2. Project onto $xy$-plane ($z=0$): $ds = \frac{dx dy}{|\hat{n} \cdot \hat{k}|} = \frac{dx dy}{1/\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3} dx dy$.
  3. Substitute $z = 1-x-y$ into $\vec{F}$.
  4. Evaluate double integral over triangle in $xy$-plane.

Case 4: Green's Theorem (Triangle/Rectangle)

$\oint (xy + y^2) dx + x^2 dy$ over triangle boundaries.

  1. Identify $M = xy+y^2, N = x^2$.
  2. Calculate $\frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = 2x - (x+2y) = x-2y$.
  3. Set up double integral $\iint (x-2y) dx dy$.
  4. Determine limits based on triangle vertices.

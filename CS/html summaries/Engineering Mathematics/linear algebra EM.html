Fundamentals & Matrices

A matrix is a rectangular array of numbers arranged in rows and columns.

Trace of Matrix (Tr(A))

Sum of principal diagonal elements.

$$ \text{Tr}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ii} = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \dots + \lambda_n $$
  • $\text{Tr}(A+B) = \text{Tr}(A) + \text{Tr}(B)$
  • $\text{Tr}(kA) = k \cdot \text{Tr}(A)$
  • $\text{Tr}(AB) = \text{Tr}(BA)$

Transpose ($A^T$)

Interchange rows and columns.

$$ (AB)^T = B^T A^T $$

Conjugate Transpose ($A^\theta$ or $A^*$): Take conjugate, then transpose.

Adjoint & Inverse

Adjoint Properties

$\text{adj}(A) = [\text{Cofactors}]^T$

$$ A(\text{adj } A) = (\text{adj } A)A = |A|I_n $$ $$ |\text{adj } A| = |A|^{n-1} $$ $$ \text{adj}(\text{adj } A) = |A|^{n-2} A $$

Inverse Calculation

$$ A^{-1} = \frac{\text{adj } A}{|A|} $$
Shortcut for 2x2:
Swap main diagonal, change sign of off-diagonal.
$$ \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad-bc}\begin{bmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{bmatrix} $$

Rank ($\rho$) Logic

  • $\rho(A)$: Rank of coefficient matrix.
  • $\rho(A|B)$: Rank of augmented matrix.
  • $n$: Number of unknowns.
  • Unique: $\rho(A) = \rho(A|B) = n$
  • Infinite: $\rho(A) = \rho(A|B) < n$
  • No Solution: $\rho(A) \neq \rho(A|B)$

Rank & Vector Spaces

Definition of Rank

The number of linearly independent rows (or columns) in a matrix.

The size of the largest non-zero minor.

Nullity: Dimension of the Null Space ($AX=0$).
Rank-Nullity Theorem: Rank + Nullity = Number of Columns ($n$).

Linear Dependence

Vectors $v_1, v_2, \dots v_n$ are Linearly Dependent if the determinant of the matrix formed by them is Zero.

Otherwise, they are Linearly Independent (Basis).

Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors

Roots of the Characteristic Equation: $$ |A - \lambda I| = 0 $$

Key Properties

Sum($\lambda$) = Trace($A$)
Product($\lambda$) = Determinant($A$)
  • Eigenvalues of $A^k$ are $\lambda^k$.
  • Eigenvalues of $A^{-1}$ are $1/\lambda$.
  • Eigenvalues of triangular/diagonal matrices are the diagonal elements themselves.

Cayley-Hamilton Theorem

Every square matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation.

If $\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6 = 0$
Then $A^2 - 5A + 6I = 0$

Useful for finding $A^{-1}$ or higher powers of $A$.

1. Protocol Classification

The Data Link Layer (DLL) is divided into Logical Link Control (LLC) and Medium Access Control (MAC). The MAC sublayer manages how multiple stations share a single communication medium (Broadcast Link).

2. ALOHA Protocols

Developed at the University of Hawaii. No station is superior; any station can transmit when it has data.

Pure ALOHA

  • Logic: Send whenever data is ready.
  • Collision: Very high probability. If Ack not received after Time Out ($2 \times T_p$), wait random back-off time and retransmit.
  • Vulnerable Time: The interval where collision can occur.
$$ Vulnerable \ Time = 2 \times T_f $$ $$ Efficiency (\eta) = G \times e^{-2G} $$ $$ Max \ \eta = 18.4\% \ (at \ G=0.5) $$

Where $T_f$ is Frame Transmission Time, $G$ is traffic load.

Slotted ALOHA

  • Logic: Time is divided into discrete slots ($T_f$). Stations can only transmit at the beginning of a slot.
  • Synchronization: Required globally.
  • Advantage: Reduces vulnerable time by half.
$$ Vulnerable \ Time = T_f $$ $$ Efficiency (\eta) = G \times e^{-G} $$ $$ Max \ \eta = 36.8\% \ (at \ G=1) $$

Doubles the efficiency of Pure ALOHA.

3. Carrier Sense Multiple Access (CSMA)

"Listen before you speak." Stations sense the carrier (medium) before transmitting to reduce collisions.

Type Behavior when Channel is BUSY Behavior when Channel is IDLE
1-Persistent Continuously sense until idle. Transmit immediately (Probability = 1).
High Collision Risk
Non-Persistent Wait random time, then sense again. Transmit immediately.
Better Efficiency, High Delays
P-Persistent Continuously sense until idle. Transmit with prob $P$, wait next slot with prob $1-P$.

4. CSMA/CD (Ethernet)

Collision Detection Logic

Used in wired Ethernet (IEEE 802.3). Stations monitor the channel while transmitting. If a collision is detected, transmission stops immediately, and a Jam Signal is sent.

flowchart LR A[Start] --> B{Sense Channel} B -- Busy --> B B -- Idle --> C[Transmit & Listen] C --> D{Collision?} D -- No --> E[Success] D -- Yes --> F[Send Jam Signal] F --> G[Increment Attempts] G --> H[Binary Exp Backoff] H --> B

🛑 The Golden Rule of CSMA/CD

To detect a collision, the transmission time ($T_t$) must be at least the round-trip propagation time ($RTT$).

$$ T_t \ge 2 \times T_p $$ $$ \frac{Length}{Bandwidth} \ge 2 \times \frac{Distance}{Speed} $$

Min Frame Size ($L$) formula:

$$ L \ge 2 \times T_p \times B $$

5. Binary Exponential Back-off

Algorithm used to resolve contention after a collision.

  • 👉 Step 1: Collision occurs (Attempt $n$).
  • 👉 Step 2: Choose $K$ randomly from range $[0, 2^n - 1]$. (Max $n=10$, Cap at 1023).
  • 👉 Step 3: Wait time = $K \times \text{Slot Time}$.
  • 👉 Note: Slot Time $\approx$ RTT. Max attempts = 15 or 16 (then packet is dropped/purged).

Capture Effect: A station that wins a backoff race has a higher probability of winning the next one, potentially causing unfairness.

6. Ethernet Frame Format (IEEE 802.3)

Standard Ethernet (10 Mbps) uses Manchester Encoding (Baud Rate = 2 $\times$ Bit Rate).

Preamble 7 Bytes 101010...
SFD 1 Byte 10101011
Dest. MAC 6 Bytes
Source MAC 6 Bytes
Len 2 Bytes
Data / Payload 46 - 1500 Bytes
CRC 4 Bytes

Data Constraints

  • Min Data: 46 Bytes (Padding added if less).
  • Max Data: 1500 Bytes (MTU).
  • Min Frame Size: 64 Bytes ($6+6+2+46+4$).
  • Max Frame Size: 1518 Bytes.

MAC Address (48-bit)

  • Unicast: 1st byte LSB is 0 (e.g., ...0).
  • Multicast: 1st byte LSB is 1 (e.g., ...1).
  • Broadcast: All bits 1 (FF:FF:FF:FF:FF:FF).

7. Efficiency of CSMA/CD

Efficiency depends on the ratio of Propagation Delay ($T_p$) to Transmission Delay ($T_t$), denoted as $a$.

$$ \eta = \frac{1}{1 + 6.44a} $$ where $a = \frac{T_p}{T_t}$
Efficiency increases if:
  • Frame Size ($L$) ↑ (increases $T_t$)
  • Bandwidth ($B$) ↓
  • Distance ($d$) ↓ (decreases $T_p$)

Ethernet is not suitable for satellite networks (High $T_p$) or very small packets.

8. Problem Solving (GATE Examples)

Q1. Min Frame Size Calculation (GATE 2005)

Given: Bandwidth $B = 1$ Gbps, Distance $d = 1$ km, Speed $v = 200,000$ km/s.
Find: Minimum frame size in bits.

Solution:

$$ T_p = \frac{1 \text{ km}}{200,000 \text{ km/s}} = 5 \times 10^{-6} \text{ sec} $$

$$ L \ge 2 \times T_p \times B $$

$$ L \ge 2 \times (5 \times 10^{-6}) \times 10^9 $$

$$ L \ge 10,000 \text{ bits} $$

Q2. Signal Speed Calculation (GATE 2015)

Given: $B = 100$ Mbps, $d = 1$ km, Min Frame Size $L = 1250$ Bytes.
Find: Signal Speed ($v$).

Solution:

Convert $L$ to bits: $1250 \times 8 = 10,000$ bits.

$$ L = 2 \times T_p \times B \implies T_p = \frac{L}{2B} $$

$$ T_p = \frac{10^4}{2 \times 10^8} = \frac{1}{2 \times 10^4} \text{ sec} $$

$$ v = \frac{d}{T_p} = \frac{1 \text{ km}}{1 / (20,000)} = 20,000 \text{ km/s} $$

Q3. Efficiency of Polling

Given: $T_{poll} = 80 \mu s$, Frame Size $= 1000$ Bytes, Capacity $= 10$ Mbps.
Find: Max Throughput.

Solution:

$$ T_t = \frac{1000 \times 8}{10 \times 10^6} = 800 \mu s $$

$$ \eta = \frac{T_t}{T_{poll} + T_t} = \frac{800}{80 + 800} = \frac{800}{880} = \frac{10}{11} $$

$$ Throughput = \eta \times B = \frac{10}{11} \times 10 \text{ Mbps} = \frac{100}{11} \text{ Mbps} $$

Q4. Maximum Stations in Slotted ALOHA

Given: Probability $p=0.2$, Min Throughput required per host $= 0.16$.
Find: Max number of hosts ($N$).

Solution:

Throughput per host = $p(1-p)^{N-1}$

$$ 0.16 = 0.2 \times (0.8)^{N-1} $$

$$ 0.8 = (0.8)^{N-1} \implies N-1 = 1 \implies N=2 $$

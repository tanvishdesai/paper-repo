Multivariable Calculus

Comprehensive notes on Double Integrals, Order Change, Curve Lengths, and Optimization of Two Variables.

Double Integration Concept

The double integral represents the volume under the surface $z = f(x,y)$ over a region $R$ in the $xy$-plane.

$$ Volume = \iint_R f(x,y) \, dx \, dy $$ $$ Area = \iint_R 1 \, dx \, dy $$

Integration Process

It involves two independent variables ($x, y$) and one dependent variable ($z$). Integration is performed sequentially:

  1. Inner Integral: Integrate with respect to the variable strip chosen (limits are functions).
  2. Outer Integral: Integrate with respect to the remaining variable (limits are constants).

Finding Limits: Vertical vs Horizontal Strips

The order of integration ($dy dx$ or $dx dy$) determines how you define the limits.

Method Vertical Strip ($dy \, dx$) Horizontal Strip ($dx \, dy$)
Inner Limit Variable $y$ goes from $y_1 = f_1(x)$ to $y_2 = f_2(x)$ (Bottom to Top) Variable $x$ goes from $x_1 = g_1(y)$ to $x_2 = g_2(y)$ (Left to Right)
Outer Limit Constant $x$ goes from $a$ to $b$ (Left to Right scan) Constant $y$ goes from $c$ to $d$ (Bottom to Top scan)
Integral Form $$ \int_{a}^{b} \left[ \int_{f_1(x)}^{f_2(x)} f(x,y) \, dy \right] dx $$ $$ \int_{c}^{d} \left[ \int_{g_1(y)}^{g_2(y)} f(x,y) \, dx \right] dy $$

Change of Order of Integration

Sometimes an integral is impossible to solve in the given order (e.g., $\int e^{x^2} dx$). We must change the order ($dy dx \to dx dy$) to solve it.

graph LR A[Start: Given Integral] --> B[Step 1: Plot the Limits] B --> C[Step 2: Identify Current Strip] C --> D[Step 3: Draw NEW Strip (Rotate 90°)] D --> E[Step 4: Find New Limits] E --> F[Evaluate New Integral] style D fill:#f43f5e,color:white
Example: $\int_0^1 \int_y^1 e^{x^2} dx dy$ (Why we need this)

Problem: $\int e^{x^2} dx$ has no elementary antiderivative. We cannot integrate $x$ first.

Limits: Inner: $x=y$ to $x=1$. Outer: $y=0$ to $y=1$.

Region: Triangle bounded by $y=0$ (x-axis), $x=1$ (vertical line), and $y=x$ (diagonal).

Change Strip: Originally Horizontal ($x$ varies). Change to Vertical ($y$ varies first).

New Limits:
Vertical strip starts at $y=0$ and ends at $y=x$.
Then $x$ runs from $0$ to $1$.

New Integral: $$ \int_0^1 \left[ \int_0^x e^{x^2} dy \right] dx $$

Solve: $\int_0^x dy = y |_0^x = x$.
Integral becomes $\int_0^1 x e^{x^2} dx$.
Let $x^2 = t \implies 2xdx = dt$. Answer: $\frac{1}{2}(e-1)$.

Length of Curve (Arc Length)

Calculates the length of a segment of a curve using integration.

Case 1: Cartesian Form ($y = f(x)$)

$$ L = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2} \, dx $$

Use when y is given as a function of x.

Case 2: Parametric Form ($x=f(t), y=g(t)$)

$$ L = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2} \, dt $$

Example: Cycloid, Circle, Ellipse.

Note: For $x = f(y)$, swap variables: $L = \int \sqrt{1 + (dx/dy)^2} \, dy$.

Maxima and Minima (Two Variables)

To find local extrema for $z = f(x,y)$.

1. Standard Notation

  • $p = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}$
  • $q = \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}$
  • $r = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}$
  • $s = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y}$
  • $t = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}$

2. The Algorithm

  1. Find Stationary Points: Solve $p = 0$ and $q = 0$.
  2. Calculate Discriminant: $\Delta = rt - s^2$ at these points.
  3. Apply the Test Condition.

Lagrange's Method (Constrained Optimization):
For finding extrema of $f(x,y)$ subject to constraint $\phi(x,y) = 0$:
Form function $F(x,y,\lambda) = f(x,y) + \lambda \phi(x,y)$ and solve $\nabla F = 0$.

Solved Illustrations

Q1: Value of $\int \int xy \, dx \, dy$ over region $x+2y=2, x=0, y=0$

Region: Triangle with vertices $(0,0), (2,0), (0,1)$.

Using Horizontal Strip:

  • Inner Limit ($x$): $0$ to $2-2y$.
  • Outer Limit ($y$): $0$ to $1$.

Integral: $$ \int_0^1 \left[ \int_0^{2-2y} x \, dx \right] y \, dy $$ $$ = \int_0^1 y \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} \right]_0^{2-2y} dy $$ $$ = \int_0^1 y \frac{(2-2y)^2}{2} dy $$ Evaluate to get result: 1/6.

Q2: Change Order: $\int_0^{\pi} \int_0^x \frac{\sin y}{\pi - y} dy dx$

Original Limits: $y: 0 \to x$, $x: 0 \to \pi$. (Vertical Strip).

Integrand: $\frac{\sin y}{\pi - y}$ is constant w.r.t $x$.

New Limits (Horizontal Strip):
$x$ goes from $y$ to $\pi$.
$y$ goes from $0$ to $\pi$.

New Integral: $$ \int_0^{\pi} \frac{\sin y}{\pi - y} \left[ \int_y^{\pi} dx \right] dy $$ $$ = \int_0^{\pi} \frac{\sin y}{\pi - y} (\pi - y) \, dy $$ $$ = \int_0^{\pi} \sin y \, dy = [-\cos y]_0^{\pi} = -(-1) - (-1) = 2 $$

Q3: Maxima/Minima of $f(x,y) = x^2 - y^2$ at (0,0)

Derivatives:
$p = 2x, q = -2y$. Stationary point at $(0,0)$.
$r = 2, s = 0, t = -2$.

Discriminant:
$rt - s^2 = (2)(-2) - 0 = -4$.

Conclusion:
Since $rt - s^2 < 0$, it is a Saddle Point. (Neither max nor min).
